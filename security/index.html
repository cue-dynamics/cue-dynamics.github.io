Security

Last updated: September 30, 2026

1. Disclosure

Please report security issues to [email protected] so we can fix them before they're made public, and we encourage you to look for them. Include the affected URL or service, the steps to reproduce the issue, and the impact you observed. We don't run a public bug bounty program and don't offer monetary rewards. If you enjoy this kind of work, take a look at our open positions.

2. What does not qualify?

Not every missing security measure is a vulnerability. For example, a static page that doesn't set X-Frame-Options probably isn't vulnerable to clickjacking, because it has no sensitive action to trick a user into. Please confirm that your finding lets an attacker do something they otherwise couldn't, such as making a data-modifying request on behalf of another user or accessing data they aren't authorized to see. We still read reports of missing hardening, but unless it's part of a larger vulnerability we treat it as low severity.

The following don't qualify:

  • Reports without written steps to reproduce the issue. A video alone isn't enough.
  • Issues that don't affect the latest versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge, or that depend on browser extensions.
  • Issues that expose public or non-sensitive information, like confirming whether an email address has an account.
  • Missing security headers, cookie flags or email authentication records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) without a demonstrated attack.
  • Issues that have already been reported or that we're aware of.
  • Issues in services we don't host ourselves, unless they're caused by a misconfiguration on our side.
  • Behavior we've deliberately accepted as a risk, usually as a trade-off for usability.

3. Rules

We want you to look for issues, but please follow these rules so nobody else's experience is disrupted:

  • Don't try to access anyone else's account or data.
  • Don't run attacks that could affect availability, like DDoS or spam.
  • Don't publicly disclose an issue before it's been fixed.
  • Don't use scanners or automated tools to find vulnerabilities. Automated scanning is noisy and may get your IP address blocked.
  • Never try non-technical attacks like social engineering, phishing, or physical attacks against our employees, users or infrastructure.
  • When in doubt, ask.

As long as you follow these rules, a human will reply within 1–3 business days and keep you updated while we work on a fix. We won't pursue legal action against researchers who act in good faith and follow this policy.

4. Scope

We welcome reports on the services we host under these domains:

  • generalintuition.com and its subdomains
  • generalintuition.ai and its subdomains
  • gi.io and its subdomains

The following are hosted by third parties and are out of scope. Please report issues with them to the provider directly:

  • Our job listings at generalintuition.com/careers, hosted by Ashby.
  • Our uptime status page at status.generalintuition.com, hosted by UptimeRobot.
